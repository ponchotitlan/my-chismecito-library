# Writing style: Technical but accessible practitioner

- **Language:** English
- **Tone:** Practical and credible, grounded in real problems
- **Voice:** Second person ("you'll"), conversational yet professional
- **Sentence length:** Mix of short punchy statements and longer explanatory sentences
- **Humor:** Minimal, relies on relatable metaphors (Tower of Babel)
- **Formatting:** Plain text with clear paragraph breaks, structured flow

## Title patterns
- Use vivid metaphors or contrasts to frame the problem space
- Include the specific technology or project name
- Longer titles with subtitles using colons are acceptable when they add clarity
- Front-load the hook, back-load the specifics

## Abstract structure
1. Open with the problem statement using concrete imagery or metaphor
2. State the consequence of the problem (what goes wrong)
3. Introduce the solution (the tool/framework/project) with its organizational backing
4. Explain the core components or approach in accessible terms
5. Describe what will happen in the session (live demo, hands-on elements)
6. Close with tangible takeaways and audience prerequisites
7. End with practical next steps (code availability, follow-along options)

## Prefer
- Problem-first framing that practitioners immediately recognize
- Concrete consequences over abstract benefits
- Lists of capabilities or components with parallel structure
- Active verbs and direct address to the audience
- Specific, verifiable takeaways ("you'll leave with X")
- Transparency about prerequisites and skill level needed
- Promise of hands-on or visual demonstration

## Avoid
- Starting with "In this talk/session, I will..."
- Buzzword stacking without explanation
- Vague promises ("learn best practices")
- Academic or overly formal language
- Skipping the "why should I care" moment
- Hiding technical requirements

## Guardrails
- Keep it technical but never assume deep expertise
- Use metaphors sparingly and only when they genuinely clarify
- Always explain what attendees get (code, checklist, understanding)

## Notes
This style works well for technical conference talks aimed at practitioners who want to evaluate whether a tool or approach fits their needs. It balances honesty about complexity with enthusiasm for solutions, and always gives people something tangible to take home.
